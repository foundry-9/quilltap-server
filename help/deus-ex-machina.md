# Deus Ex Machina — declaring a thing true

An announcement is *said.* An inform is *known.* A Deus Ex Machina is *true.*

Every author, sooner or later, wants to reach into the scene and simply settle a matter. The bridge to Ashford collapsed in the night. The letter was a forgery. It has been raining for three days. Nobody is to argue about it, nobody is to doubt it, and nobody — least of all the character who remembers things rather differently — is to ask for proof. The Salon offers two instruments for the purpose, and both go by the name of the old stage machine that lowered a god onto the boards to tidy up the plot.

## In your own voice: `/deus` and the shortcut

The line you are about to send can be posted *as established fact.* It goes out under your own seat, with your own name and avatar, exactly as you typed it — but it arrives marked as something true in the story, known to everyone present, and in need of no verification.

There are three ways to do it:

- **Press the little screen on your portrait.** In the bottom-left corner of the *speaking as* portrait beside the composer sits a small button bearing a game-master's screen — the folding panel behind which the one who runs the table decides what is so. (Under the Madman's Box theme it is an eye in a triangle instead, the all-seeing narrator.) Hover over it and it introduces itself. Press it and the composer is armed, exactly as `/deus` arms it; press it again to stand it down. While armed, the button wears the same ring and tint as the editor, so the two always agree. It stays bright and pressable even while a reply is still arriving and the portrait has dimmed. (On the narrowest screens the portrait folds away to make room, and the button with it; `/deus` and the shortcut still serve.)
- **Type `/deus`** in the composer and pick **Deus Ex Machina** from the menu. The composer is now *armed*: a chip appears above it reading *Deus Ex Machina: this line will be established as fact*, the editor takes on a faint tint, and the Send button's label changes to *Send as established fact.* Send your line and the composer disarms itself. Pick `/deus` a second time, press the portrait button again, or press the chip's **×**, to disarm without sending. Switching chats or reloading the page disarms it too.
- **Press ⌘ + Shift + Enter** (Ctrl + Shift + Enter away from a Mac) to send just this one line as fact without arming anything. It works in chat mode and in composition mode alike.

A fact needs words. A composer holding only an attachment cannot be armed, and an armed send with nothing written is politely refused.

Your line keeps its exact wording. If you are impersonating a character with *In Their Own Words* switched on, an established fact skips the rehearsal entirely: the words you wrote are the words that are true, and nobody is invited to improve upon them.

In the transcript the bubble wears a small ribbon — *Deus Ex Machina — established as fact* — so you can find it again later.

## From the machine: the Deus Ex Machina tab

Sometimes the fact belongs to nobody at all. Open **Insert Announcement** (the megaphone in the composer gutter) and choose its fourth tab, **Deus Ex Machina**. The speaker pickers, the in-character rewrite and the *Who hears it* list all fold away; what is left is a single box marked *Fact* and a button marked *Establish*.

The result appears in the chat as a full-width band labelled *Deus Ex Machina*, open by default and never tucked away among the Host's smaller chips.

## What the cast actually hears

**Never the name.** "Deus Ex Machina" is a label for you, in the Salon, and nowhere else. No character, summariser, memory clerk, title writer or Courier bundle ever reads it. What they read is plain: *established fact — true in this story, known to everyone, needing no verification.*

**Your own line** reaches them as your line, prefaced by that note, with your name on it as usual.

**A fact from the machine** depends on the room. Where every character has [System Transparency](character-system-transparency.md) switched on, the Host delivers it: *The Host makes it known, as established fact…* Where even one character keeps their transparency off, the whole room hears it with no speaker at all — one shared reality, the same rule the Staff's names already follow.

## Who knows it

**Everyone, always, and in public.** There is no whispered Deus Ex Machina; private knowledge is what [Inform](inform.md) is for. The server refuses an established fact with an audience.

**Absent and silent characters know it.** So does a character who joins the conversation afterwards, even one who was not granted the chat's earlier history: an established fact slips past the usual join-time cut, because everybody knows it.

## The reminder on their next turn

A fact posted three lines up can still be out-argued by a memory recalled in the same breath. So, on each character's next turn after a fact is posted, the House adds a short reminder at the very end of their prompt — *Established since you last spoke…* — listing every fact posted since that character last opened their mouth, your own lines prefixed with your name. It vouches for them in the same terms as the facts themselves, and adds that they outrank any older memory or record that disagrees.

It appears once. On the character's following turn it is gone, though the facts themselves remain in the conversation for as long as the conversation does. Regenerating or swiping a reply reproduces exactly the reminder the original reply saw. A very long list is trimmed from the oldest end; anything trimmed is still in the history.

Carina does not hear the reminder. An inline `@Name:` query builds its own small, fresh call, as it does for informs.

## Memories

Established facts are remembered, and remembered *as facts* — the summary of the conversation carries them forward as settled truth of the world rather than as anyone's claim, and the memory clerk is told the same.

- **A fact from the machine** is written straight into the Commonplace Book of every character in the chat who is not played by you and has not been removed — absent and silent characters included — word for word, as a memory you authored. Because it is yours, it is never merged into one the clerk extracted, and wiping a chat's extracted memories leaves it alone.
- **Your own line** is remembered by the characters present through the ordinary memory passes, which now read it as established fact.

Editing or deleting a fact afterwards changes the transcript but **does not unform a memory already made.** If a fact changes, put the old memory right yourself in the [Commonplace Book](memory-maintenance.md).
