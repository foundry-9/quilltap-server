# The Book Remembers When and Where

> **[Open this page in Quilltap](/settings?tab=memory)**

For the longest time, the Commonplace Book kept splendid notes on *what* happened and rather shabby ones on *when* and *where*. Ask a character "remember that place we visited last week?" and the poor thing was left rifling through undated pages — it might draw a blank, or worse, invent an answer with perfect confidence. No longer. The Book has taken up the habits of a proper diarist: every memory now carries the date the thing *happened* (not merely the date it was written down), the places and persons involved, and — for stories that keep their own calendar — the in-story hour as well.

## What Changed on the Shelves

- **Every memory is dated by the event.** A recollection formed today about last month's outing is filed under last month, where it belongs. When a character's memories are whispered into a conversation, each arrives with its age on its sleeve — *[3 days ago]*, *[last week]* — so the character can actually confirm "yes, last Tuesday" instead of guessing.
- **Outings become episodes.** A real excursion spans many turns of conversation, and used to survive only as scattered one-line fragments. Now, as the Librarian folds a chapter of your chat into the running summary, the Book composes a proper episode entry — a short, dated narrative of what happened, where, and with whom — and ties the fragments to it with ribbon. The Book keeps a bookmark of how far its episodes have reached, so the periodic full re-reading of the summary (every fifty exchanges, or after an import) composes episodes only for the chapters it has not yet written up — never a second, sweeping retelling of the whole affair from the first page — and reads any long unwritten stretch a few dozen messages at a time. A stretch too long for one sitting is not abandoned should the chat go quiet: once a chat has rested a couple of hours, the nightly maintenance round resumes the Book's episode-writing from its bookmark, a few chapters per errand, until it reaches the last page the Librarian has folded — and never a page beyond it. The running summary itself gains a dated **Timeline** of events, kept in every character's vault.
- **Two visits are two memories.** Previously, visiting the same harbor in spring and again in winter could collapse into a single confused entry. The Book now checks the dates before merging: same activity, different occasion — both are kept. It checks every close match on the shelf, not merely the closest, so a retelling of last Tuesday's outing still finds last Tuesday's memory even when a near-identical spring visit sits beside it. Nor will the Book fold a note about one person into a note about another, or a dated episode into a general truth, however alike their wording: such near-twins are kept apart and tied together with ribbon instead.
- **Old memories are not punished for being old — when you ask about old times.** Ordinarily the Book favors the fresh and the current. But the moment a conversation turns retrospective ("remember when…?"), it reverses course: memories of the past are *promoted*, the referenced time period is searched directly, and the usual courtesy of not repeating itself is suspended — because when you re-ask, you want the same memory found, not politely withheld.
- **"Today" and "yesterday" are now understood without consultation.** There was an awkward interval in which a character could recall an outing of a fortnight past and yet draw a perfect blank on the expedition of that very afternoon — the phrase *"the mission today"* being read, by the Book's junior clerk, as idle present-tense chatter. The common turns of the calendar — *today, this morning, last night, yesterday, the day before yesterday, three days ago, last week* — are now resolved by the Book itself, on your own clock rather than Greenwich's, without asking the clerk's opinion at all. Say "how did it go this morning?" and the morning is what gets searched.
- **The very recent is kept warm.** Quite apart from any phrasing, a memory of something that happened within the last day or two is now given a lift in the running order, so that the events of yesterday afternoon are not quietly outranked by a settled truth from a fortnight ago. (Recollections drawn from the conversation you are presently *in* are exempted — they are already before you on the table, and need no announcement.)
- **A dated reading list, on demand.** On such retrospective turns the Book also produces a small extra whisper: the past conversations that cover the period in question, each with its date and a conversation ID the character can open with the `read_conversation` tool to reread the original scene.
- **What counts as an agreement.** Neither a turn's memories nor an episode may record an agreement unless the agreeing party's own words of assent appear in the transcript. A proposal, condition, or demand is recorded as exactly that and attributed to whoever made it; stated limits ("until breakfast," "for tonight") stay in the text; and when a proposal was the last word in the turn, the memory says the other party "had not yet responded" rather than inventing a reply. Older memories that claim an agreement nobody spoke can be re-formed under these rules with [Regenerate the Commonplace Book](memory-regenerate.md).
- **Honest tools, honest answers.** Characters' `search` tool now accepts a time period (`since`/`until`) and a subject (`aboutCharacter`), returns each memory's event date and source conversation, and `read_conversation` can fetch just a slice of a long transcript. And the standing instruction is now written plainly into every character's orders: if the search turns up nothing, say you don't recall — never invent the particulars. Should the embedding provider be out to lunch and the Book fall back on reading its pages word by word, the same rules still hold: the time period, the turn's sense of *when*, and the pages set aside for a replay are all honoured, rather than every page with a matching word tumbling out regardless of date.

## One Calendar for the Whole House

The Book now keeps a single calendar for both the filing and the finding. When a character says *"last night we walked to the lighthouse,"* the clerk dates the memory to the evening before, on your own clock; when you later ask *"do you remember last night?"* the very same evening is searched, on the very same clock. (Previously the filing clerk worked in Greenwich time and the finding clerk in yours, and the two would pass each other in the corridor without so much as a nod — a "last night" filed at midnight Greenwich fell entirely outside the "last night" asked for.) *Last week* now means the previous Monday-to-Sunday on both sides of the desk, and a date the Book's junior clerk names without an hour is read as your whole day, not Greenwich's. The two clerks now read from one shared table of phrases — *today*, *this morning*, *yesterday*, *three days ago*, as far back as *fourteen days ago* — so a phrase cannot mean one thing at the filing desk and another at the finding desk. A memory filed as happening *today* is stamped at the start of today, on your clock, so it sits squarely inside the window a later *"today"* searches; and every window closes a hair before the next one opens, so *yesterday* ends exactly where *today* begins and no memory is ever counted in both.

Which clock? The **Default timezone** in the Salon's settings, if you have set one; failing that, the `QUILLTAP_TIMEZONE` the instance was started with; failing *that*, the machine's own. A chat's own timestamp timezone governs only how times are *displayed* in that chat — it does not move the Book's calendar.

Memories filed *before* this change were given Greenwich midnights, which west of Greenwich would have read as the evening before. On the first start after the upgrade the Book quietly moves each of those to midnight of the same day on your own clock, so an older memory dated the fourteenth stays dated the fourteenth, whichever side of Greenwich you live on. Dates carrying an actual hour — the moment a turn was spoken, say — are left exactly as they were, and new memories are filed on your calendar from the start. The move is made once, against the clock in force at the time; changing the **Default timezone** afterwards does not re-file anything.

## Stories on Their Own Calendar

If your chat is a work of fiction that runs on its own clock — the third night at sea, the eve of the coronation — the Book can keep *that* calendar too. A chat may be set to **narrative** timeline mode (the default is **realtime**), in which case in-story time phrases are preserved alongside the wall-clock date and shown with each memory.

The switch lives in the Salon: open the chat sidebar, unfold the **Chat** card, and find **The Story's Clock**. *Real time* files memories by the calendar on the wall; *Story time* lets the narrative's own reckoning govern. Flip it whenever you please — the setting is per chat, takes effect on the next turn, and memories already on the shelves keep whatever dates they were filed under.

## For the Mechanically Inclined

The `quilltap recall-replay <chatId>` command (with the dev server running) replays any turn's memory recall and prints the full ranking table — old behavior and new, side by side — so you can see precisely why a given memory surfaced or sank. For the patient tinkerer it will also try out new recall constants on the newer column alone (`--tuning`), reuse a turn's distilled question from an earlier run so two replays ask exactly the same thing (`--signals-from`), and consult only the shelves as they stood when the turn was spoken (`--as-of`). After the two tables it shows what the plain-text fallback would have offered, and a stage-by-stage account of the turn — what the steady shelf held, what the token budget cut, what the section about the others took — with the Book's record of recent whispers restored as it stood at that turn. See the [CLI reference](cli-memories.md) for its sibling tools.
