*4.12 made your word law. You could post a line as established fact, and every character in the house would know it whether or not they had been in the room. The shelves got one set of labels, and the search bar learned to look in the room you were standing in.*

*What 4.12 could not do was tell the house what anything was. Name the* Marigold *in passing, and nobody knew she was a ship, let alone a blue-hulled ketch with a cracked figurehead. A butler and a research assistant were built from the same parts, so the butler negotiated with burglars in the tone of a helpdesk. The settings were eight tabs named after the staff, and you had to know who was in charge of a thing before you could find it. And a new chat made you wait behind a dialog while three model calls happened in a back room.*

*4.13 is the release in which the house learns what things are: the things that are spoken of, the people who speak, and where everything is kept.*

---

Where 4.12 let you say "so it is," 4.13 lets the house know what it is talking about.

**The Atlas** is the headline. It is a new place, in every tier from a character's own vault to Quilltap General, for named things that are not characters: a ship, a sword, a tavern, a town, a secret society. Each entry has a name, aliases, a short description of how it looks, and whatever is known about it. When one is named in the Salon, the character who answers is told about it, a chip appears on the name for you, and the Lantern paints it as described rather than guessing.

Four further bodies of work define the cycle.

**Characters know what kind they are.** Every character is now a **roleplay** character (a person inside a story with stakes) or an **assistant** (someone whose job is doing work for you). Agent Mode, the tool rules, the sample prompts and the prompt generators all follow the kind. New sample prompts, new direction for the generators, and an Inform that can carry feelings as well as facts teach story characters to meet danger at full size.

**Aurora puts each character on one page.** View and Edit are gone; one page in six sections saves itself as you type. Characters gain birthdays and public progressions, and a vault that cannot be read gets a repair card instead of an error.

**The Foundry is rebuilt.** Settings are now ten groups named for what they do, with search, a lens for each member of the staff, an attention strip for things that need you, and connections organised by provider.

**The curtain goes up at once.** Creating a chat no longer waits on any model. The chat opens immediately, the greeting streams in the Salon like any other turn, and the Green Room narrates every arrival, including characters who join halfway through, and now packs their pockets as well as dressing them.

A good many more earned their places. You can **edit any line** in the Salon, and you are told whether a model has already read it and which memories it formed. **Turns mode** runs one full round and stops. Every bubble keeps **the face its speaker wore** when it was written. The Wardrobe gains a **portrait-cue polisher**. And **prompt caching** on OpenRouter, Claude, NanoGPT and Z.AI now saves money as it always claimed to.

A few things change in ways worth reading before you upgrade, all under **Upgrading**: every existing character becomes a **roleplay** character, which changes how Agent Mode behaves for your assistants until you set their kind; the **MODERN General** sample prompt is retired; the Brahma Console's default **turn budget** drops from 50 to 10; and anyone who creates chats through the **API** must now start the opening themselves.

The thesis is simple: *4.12 let you say "so it is." 4.13 lets the house know what it is talking about.*

## The Atlas

### Named Things

Until now, Quilltap knew a great deal about the people in a story and nothing at all about the things. A character's vault could hold pages of lore, but that lore reached the conversation only if someone thought to go and read it.

**The Atlas** is a new folder in each of the four tiers: a character's vault, a group's store, a project's store and Quilltap General. An entry is a single Markdown file with:

- a **name**, unique in its tier, and any number of **aliases** (*the Marigold*, *the old ketch*);
- a **graphic prompt**, a short description of how the thing looks, for the painters (a gentle warning appears past 200 characters);
- **knowledge**, in Markdown: what is known about it;
- optionally, a **picture**, uploaded or generated.

The Atlas has a button in the left sidebar, after the Inventory, and opens as a tab of its own. It also has a card in Prospero's settings and on a character's Appearance section. It uses the same folder browser as the Wardrobe and the Inventory: folders, search, move, copy, duplicate, and transfers between tiers. As everywhere else, the nearest tier wins. A character's own entry for *the Marigold* shadows the project's entry of the same name.

### Told When Named

When a thing in the Atlas is named in the recent conversation, the character about to answer is told about it in a short section headed *What is being spoken of*, provided the entry is within that character's reach. Matching is by whole word and ignores case. A name beats an alias, and the longest match wins. Words that any character in the workspace answers to are never treated as things, so a character called Rook does not set off the Atlas entry for the chess piece. On a character's first turn under a scenario, things the scenario names are included too.

At most eight entries ride along on a turn. Nothing is stored and nothing is remembered; when a thing stops being mentioned, it stops being sent. A turn that names nothing in the Atlas sends exactly what it sent before.

### Chips for You

Things named in the transcript, and in the Host's scenario announcements, wear a chip, as characters' names already do. A thing's chip is styled differently from a person's. Its popup shows the picture, the aliases, the graphic prompt, the knowledge and the tier the entry came from. Where a person's name and a thing's name overlap, the person wins.

### In the Pictures

The Lantern now knows what named things look like. A story background is painted with the looks of every known thing in the scene, the scenario or the recent conversation. `generate_image` does the same, whether it is called by a character, from an image profile, or on the uncensored retry. A `{{Name}}` placeholder that names only an Atlas entry is replaced with the entry's name, and if the prompt crafter fails, the looks are added to the prompt anyway. A prompt that names nothing in the Atlas is left unchanged. Avatars, wardrobe pictures and Inventory pictures never consult the Atlas.

An entry can have pictures of its own, uploaded or generated (generation counts toward image spend). A picture opens full screen from its thumbnail and can be **saved to a document store**, as Wardrobe and Inventory pictures can.

### Where It Lives

The Atlas folder in a character's vault is part of the vault's core, so a character without system transparency cannot read or rewrite it with her document tools. Entries travel with backups and restores, and their pictures stay attached.

## What Kind of Character

### Roleplay or Assistant

The cycle began with an incident. In two chats on the same evening, an armed intruder walked into a house full of characters, and every one of them met him with calm negotiation. One offered him a tour. The request logs traced it to the prompts: companion-framed stock prompts, instructions to keep things small and soothing, generated prompts that said things like "never tense", and Agent Mode wording written for a research assistant.

The remedy starts with a fact that should have been there all along. Every character now has a **kind**: **roleplay**, the default, for a person inside a story with stakes; or **assistant**, for a character whose job is doing work for you. Set it on the *Kind of character* control in the Facts card on the character page. It travels in `.qtap` exports and imports.

### Agent Mode Follows the Kind

Agent Mode now comes in two flavours, chosen by the kind of the character answering. Your settings still decide whether it is on.

- A **roleplay** character gets **in-character** Agent Mode. Tools are things she does in the scene, the turn is still written in character as prose, and she is never offered a "submit final response" button. When she reaches the step limit, she is told to put the tools down and write her turn.
- An **assistant** gets the research loop, unchanged.

The rules that come with native tools follow the kind too. A roleplay character's say how to use tools; they no longer claim to override who she is. The Brahma Console, Help chat and the other one-shot tool loops always use the assistant's form. The Salon's Agent Mode cap goes up from 25 turns to 50.

### Matching the Stakes

The sample prompts are rewritten around stakes.

- **MODERN Roleplay** is the new recommended prompt for roleplay characters. It adds sections on reacting to danger and on group scenes, and names two failure modes: *flattening* (meeting danger with small talk) and *the mediator*.
- **MODERN Assistant** is for characters whose job is work.
- **MODERN General** is retired.
- Every companion, romantic and platonic sample gains a short passage on matching the stakes. Ordinary days stay ordinary, real danger gets real fear, anger or action, and a character's usual temperament is not a vow. DeepSeek's style governors still rein in purple prose but no longer tell the character to keep the story small.
- Every story prompt now forbids **inventing where you are**. In testing, characters decided your persona had gone down to the boathouse and planned around it for several turns. Now a character may wonder aloud, call out, or go and look.

The prompt writers follow the kind too. The **AI Wizard**, **Summon From Lore**, the **Character Optimizer** and the **External Prompt** generator give a roleplay character stakes and never write her temperament as an absolute. They give an assistant accuracy, no invented facts and no padding. They read the kind you set and never guess it from source material, since household staff look like assistants to a model. The Optimizer now treats flattening in a roleplay character's memories as drift to correct. The AI Wizard's system prompt also gets more room, so a reasoning model no longer hands back a prompt cut off mid-sentence.

### The Cast Need Not Agree

The rules every speaker in a multi-character chat receives now say plainly that the cast does not have to agree. If everyone else took the measured view, a character may answer with fear, anger or action, and should never take a position only because the others have.

An **Inform** can now carry feelings. Its header tells the character it is hearing what has happened, what she knows, and how she feels or what she has resolved, and that where this conflicts with an older memory, her records or her usual temperament, the Inform is the current truth. Before, "You are very alarmed" was filed as a fact and outweighed by her usual calm.

### Refine from Memories & Instructions

The Character Optimizer's *Refine from Memories* is now **Refine from Memories & Instructions**. Its first form has a box for your own instructions (up to 8,000 characters) and a *Consult the Commonplace Book* checkbox. A run can use memories, instructions, or both. Your instructions reach every pass, from general fields and scenarios to prompts, physical description, wardrobe and aliases, so a change you ask for is made wherever it applies. They win over conflicting memories, but never over the rules that keep the fields distinct or the character safe. The analysis lists each change your instructions called for. A run with too few memories now carries on with your instructions instead of stopping.

## Aurora, on One Page

### One Page That Saves Itself

A character used to have two pages, one to look at and one to edit, and you had to remember to press Save. Now there is **one character page** at `/aurora/<id>`, in six sections: **Details**, **Prompts**, **Appearance**, **Abilities**, **Chat Defaults** and **History**. There is no Save button outside a dialog. Prose saves after a second and a half of quiet or when you leave the field; short text saves on Enter or leaving the field; toggles, menus and stars save when changed. If a save fails, the text stays, the field is outlined, and the header offers Retry. Anything unsaved is saved when you switch tabs or close the window.

The header holds the avatar menu (choose from photos, upload, or generate a portrait through the Wardrobe), badges for kind, NPC, user control, Carina and archive, statistics that link to what they count, **Start Chat**, a **Generate** menu (AI Wizard, Optimizer, Non-Quilltap prompt) and a **⋯** menu (Export, Convert to NPC, Archive and, new on this page, Delete). The header's avatar now fills its height, and its menus open where you clicked; before, they appeared down and to the right.

Throughout the page:

- Empty prose fields show their hint as a placeholder, and `{{char}}`, `{{user}}` and the names that could become them are tinted inside the editors.
- The system-prompt star is the only way to choose the default prompt. Scenarios are a list with a dialog, each with a star for the default.
- A new **Metadata** card lists a character's metadata keys and edits them as JSON. It and the Progressions card save only their own keys, so neither can overwrite the other's.
- The header now counts what the character carries: objects, defaults, archetypes and kits, each opening the Inventory.
- History's Conversations and Memories start folded and page with Previous and Next instead of scrolling forever.

Old addresses redirect, `?tab=` becomes `?section=`, and saved workspace layouts are upgraded on load, with a view tab and an edit tab for the same character merged into one.

This page also fixes **#146**. The edit form's System Prompt field, the create form's System Prompt and Scenario fields, and the AI Wizard's generated prompt were all being thrown away silently on save. They are now saved as a real prompt (and scenario), and the New Character form also saves aliases, pronouns, birthday and kind.

### When the Vault Is in Disarray

A character whose files cannot be read used to show "could not be loaded" and nothing else. The page now shows a **vault-health card** instead. It lists each file as present or absent, gives the parse error for any present file that is broken, and offers **Restore** (which writes only the files that are missing) and **Look again**.

Behind it, three ways of losing work are closed:

- **#148.** At startup, a character whose settings file was missing or invalid had her whole vault rewritten from an empty record: identity, description, manifesto, personality and example dialogues blanked, and every prompt and scenario swept away. The startup check now writes a missing file and nothing else. An invalid file is reported and left alone for you to look at.
- **#149.** The physical description's **Clear** button did nothing. It clears now.
- **#150.** Saving all of a character's scenarios at once (the Optimizer's apply, the template tools) un-archived archived scenarios and dropped their descriptions. They are kept now. A hand-written note in the Prompts or Scenarios folder that isn't a prompt or scenario is no longer swept away on save.

Prompts and scenarios also now keep the **same identity when renamed**, so a chat or a default that points at one still finds it.

### Birthdays and Public Progressions

A character can have a **birthday**. The date picker sits under Pronouns, and her age is worked out on the instance calendar, never stored, and shown beside her pronouns in the header. A leap-day birthday turns over on the first of March in other years.

A **progression** can now be **public** (the default) or private. The editor previews both the line she reads and the line others see. Public progressions, her age and her pronouns now form her **public identity**, which is what the Host announces when she joins, what the name-chip card shows, what Carina's askers are told, and what Brahma's character lookup returns. A progression that has finished for good is left out. Her own system prompt is unchanged.

A character has exactly one physical description, so its leftover **name** and **usage context** fields are gone. Neither was ever stored. Her appearance line now reads simply *This is how you look: …*.

### Her Own Drawers

A character without system transparency used to be shut out of every vault, her own included. She may now use **her own vault**, except its core. Her root-level files and her Prompts, Wardrobe, Outfits, Inventory, Atlas, Mail and images folders stay closed to her document tools, but Scenarios, Knowledge, Tools, lore and any folder she makes are hers to read and write. Other characters' vaults stay closed.

## The Foundry, Rebuilt

The Settings page had eight tabs named for the staff who looked after them, with 56 cards between them, 21 of them under Chat. API keys lived in one tab and the profiles that used them in three others. Two help pages linked to sections that did not exist, and there was no search.

The page is now **The Foundry**, organised in **ten groups** named for what the settings do, in a rail down the left (a menu on a narrow screen):

**Connections**, **Composer & Display**, **Context & Memory**, **Characters & Prompts**, **Images**, **Games & Tools**, **Moderation**, **Appearance**, **Security**, and **Data & Maintenance**.

Rarely used cards sit below an *Advanced* divider in each group.

- **Search.** Press `/` and type. Cards are matched by title, description, keywords, group and the name of the staff member responsible, and matches are highlighted. Escape clears the search and puts you back where you were.
- **The staff as lenses.** Every card wears its owner's badge. A row of portraits above the rail narrows the whole page to one member of the staff, with their backdrop behind it. Brahma joins the staff with art of their own, and Madman's Box gains backdrops for the Concierge, Pascal, Saquel and Brahma.
- **Things wanting attention.** A strip at the top lights up for a profile with no key or a missing provider plugin, the Concierge off duty, memories awaiting backfill, a failed background task, or a plugin that would not load. Opening Settings with nothing else asked for goes to the first lit item's group.
- **Connections by provider.** One card for each provider you use holds its key and every connection, image and embedding profile that depends on it, with an *Add* menu that fills in the provider for you. A *By kind* toggle brings back the old four lists.

Every old Settings link, `?tab=` and all, still lands in the right place, and will keep doing so.

## The Curtain Goes Up

### The Chat Opens at Once

Creating a chat used to be a single long request that dressed everyone, introduced the scene and wrote the greeting, three model calls in all, behind a progress dialog you could not dismiss. Now creating a chat only **casts** it, and returns as soon as the chat exists. The **opening** then runs in the Salon, where you can watch it:

- The Host's scenario and introductions, the arrivals and the greeting stream in as they happen.
- The greeting is an **ordinary turn**. It gets the same recall, Informs, Inventory, progressions, fallback, Concierge routing and stall watchdog as any other turn, so it reads like the rest of the conversation instead of like a form letter.
- **Stop** skips the greeting and leaves the chat ready for you. If you send a line, it waits for the opening to finish. An opening interrupted by a restart picks up where it left off.

Chats also no longer begin with a hidden system-prompt row that every reader had to skip.

The **New Chat** dialog sends only what you changed, and the server fills in the rest the same way the dialog showed it. Two disagreements between them are fixed: leaving the outfit choice to the API used to dress a character who may choose for herself in her defaults (**#152**), and a per-seat image profile that was documented as ignored was being used (**#154**).

### The Green Room, in the Salon

The Green Room is now a panel above the transcript, narrating who is being dressed and packed and how it went, and folding to one line when the curtain is up. It can be **closed**: it then docks as a folded card in the transcript at the point where it began, and scrolls with the conversation. A later Green Room docks the earlier one instead of discarding it.

Every arrival now goes through the same steps in the same order: dress, pack, take her place, the Host's welcome, Aurora's note on what she chose to wear, and a fresh portrait. Whether she arrives at the start, joins halfway through, or comes in when chats are merged, the same rules apply. A character added mid-chat with *Dress Themselves* used to be dressed in silence (**#151**). Her arrival is now narrated in the Salon.

### Packing Everyone's Pockets

A character who chooses her own outfit now also chooses **what she carries**. The Green Room offers her what she can reach (marking what she usually carries), the scenario, her own fields and the outfit she has just put on, and she picks. If the model fails, takes too long or isn't set, she gets her usual defaults, as before.

The Green Room can be given **its own model**: **Green Room Wardrobe Model**, under the cheap-LLM settings in the Foundry. When it is set, that profile chooses openings instead of the cheap LLM. The outfit offer now names the pieces in each outfit set, so a character can take just the blazer from the linen suit.

## In the Salon

### Edit Any Line

The pencil now appears on **every** line: characters' lines, the Staff's announcements, Carina's answers, established facts. Opening the editor tells you two things. First, whether a model has **already read** the line, since an edit then rewrites history someone has acted on. Second, **how many memories** the old text formed, with a *regenerate on save* checkbox (on by default). When you save, those memories are retired and formed again from the new text. An edited character line now shows its new text at once instead of after a reload. In a chat with an opaque character, the edited text is what she sees.

### Turns Mode

The Salon's turn control has four positions: **Normal**, **Manual** (the old *Paused*, renamed), **Turns** and **Storyteller**. **Turns** runs the conversation until every character present has spoken or passed once, and then stops. A **Next Round** button in the sidebar starts the next round, and memories are gathered when a round closes. If the round reaches your seat partway through, it stops there as it always did, and your line counts as your turn. **Continue** now says plainly when the floor is yours, where it used to say "No characters available to speak."

### Faces Fixed to Their Moment

Every bubble used to show its speaker's **current** portrait, so a change of clothes repainted the whole conversation. Now each bubble shows the portrait its speaker had **when the line was written**. A new portrait, from a wardrobe or Inventory change or set by hand, applies from the moment it is bound to the chat. Chats from before this release keep today's portraits on their earlier lines. The portrait history travels in backups and `.qtap` exports.

### Smaller Courtesies

- **Emoji and symbol typeaheads everywhere.** `:name` and `\name` now work in every Markdown editor (character fields, prompts, scenarios, memories, the Wardrobe and Inventory editors, mail, announcements, Informs, the Scenario Builder), each still following its composer setting. The menu no longer opens underneath a dialog.
- **Tooltips let go.** A tooltip could stay up indefinitely when its trigger re-rendered, an overlay covered it, or the window lost focus. Tooltips now close when the pointer leaves, on a click elsewhere, and when the window loses focus. They no longer open after a mouse click, or when a dialog closes and returns focus to its button.
- **Brahma's working is easier to follow.** In the Brahma Console, each SQL card starts folded to one line (database, query, row count). Brahma's thinking now appears between the queries, where it actually happened, instead of in one block above the answer. The Console's turn budget no longer has an upper limit; see **Upgrading** for its new default.

## The Wardrobe

### Polish Portrait Cues

Every Wardrobe tier gains a **Polish portrait cues** wizard. The cheap LLM reads each garment's portrait cue against its title, slots and description. It fills in blank cues, fixes cues that contradict the description, and trims cues over 200 characters. You review every recommendation, edit any you like, and apply them all or only the ones you choose. Nothing is written until you do.

### Import from Image

**Import from image** now lets you choose which profile reads the photograph, from every profile that can actually receive one, preset to the one it would have chosen anyway. The review screen says which profile did the reading. Every garment it proposes now comes with a portrait cue you can edit before it is created.

### Wear Again Waits for Done

**Wear again** on the History tab used to change the character's outfit in the Salon immediately, while the Live outfit tab still showed the old one. It now **stages** the outfit like any other change. The Live tab shows it, the History tab marks it *Staged — on at Done* with an Unstage button, and nothing is sent until you press Done. If you have other staged changes, it asks before replacing them.

Wardrobe dialogs (the polisher, import from image, the item editor) no longer slide under the left rail on a narrow window.

### Pictures in the Store Browser

Every document-store browser has a **List / Pictures** toggle beside its search box. Pictures shows folders and files as square tiles, with images shown as images and the name and date laid over each tile. Each browser remembers your choice.

## Under the Floorboards

### Prompt Caches, Actually Read

4.12.2 fixed the worst of the caching waste. This cycle fixes the rest.

- **Anthropic** (plugin 1.0.68). The conversation's cache mark sometimes landed on the Staff's whispers just before your newest line. Those whispers are replaced every turn, so the entry was written and never read. Marks now go on the previous reply and on stable history. In testing, the turn after a cold start read back 19,301 tokens and wrote only 78.
- **OpenRouter** (plugin 1.0.73). Cache hits on most background tasks were never counted, because the plugin read them from a field the SDK never fills. They are counted now, along with cache writes. The per-character cache key also goes out as `session_id` and `prompt_cache_key`, so OpenRouter keeps routing a character to the same provider. **Claude through OpenRouter** never cached at all. Profiles have a new opt-in, **Enable Prompt Caching** with a **Cache Duration**, that places proper cache marks for Anthropic models.
- **NanoGPT** (plugin 1.2.15). Explicit caching now stops before your newest line, so Claude's automatic marks cannot land on the part that changes every turn. The profile help notes that explicit caching is billed per use outside a subscription.
- **Z.AI** (plugin 1.1.36) now sends the cache key in the field Z.AI actually reads (`user_id`). Whether that improves Z.AI's hit rate is still being measured. **DeepSeek** (plugin 1.0.32) now records its own cache hit and miss counts in the LLM logs.

**Tool results** in the history used to be cut to a one-line stub after three replies for every provider. Each cut changed a message partway up the history and forced Anthropic to rewrite its cache from that point; one long Opus chat paid for about 200,000 cache tokens twice for that reason alone. Connection profiles now have a setting for how many turns tool results stay whole, under *Tool format*. Existing profiles keep 3, and `0` keeps them whole forever.

### The Character Vault, Settled

Under the new character page, the way a character is stored was rebuilt so that the vault's layout is written down in one place. Every reader, writer, starter file and backup rule now follows that one record. For you this means the repairs above (#148–#150), prompts and scenarios that keep their identity through a rename, and saves that write one prompt or scenario at a time instead of rewriting the folder. Brahma's character lookup also now finds a character by alias when two characters share a name; before, that search silently found nothing.

For anyone who uses the API: the character endpoints are now **strict**. An unknown field is refused with a 400 instead of being dropped without a word. The whole-character PUT no longer takes prompts or scenarios, which have their own endpoints. There are new `?action=vault-health`, `?action=repair-vault` and `?action=patch-metadata` actions. Creating a chat (`POST /api/v1/chats`) now only casts it; the opening runs from the Salon, from an autonomous room's start, or from `?action=open`, and `?action=casting-defaults` reports what a new chat would get. See **Upgrading**.

### For Plugin and Theme Authors

`@quilltap/plugin-utils` moves to 2.7.5, and every provider plugin moves to it. theme-storybook moves from 1.0.101 to 1.0.111, picking up the classes for the Foundry (`qt-settings-*`), the Green Room panel and its docked card (`qt-green-room*`), the message editor (`qt-chat-message-editor*`), Atlas chips (`qt-mention-chip-thing`, `qt-tooltip-image`, `qt-tooltip-markdown`), the Brahma SQL card summaries and left-aligned thinking, the picture tiles, and a new `qt-text-brand` for text in the theme's primary colour. Every dialog's backdrop is now the shared `qt-dialog-overlay`: it sits above the left sidebar, takes the theme's backdrop colour and no longer blurs what lies beneath, and the remaining hand-drawn primary buttons now wear `qt-button-primary`, so a theme that styles either is obeyed everywhere. The typeahead menu's `z-index` rises from 50 to 200, above dialogs and below toasts. The Salon's turn control is now a two-by-two grid, so *Storyteller* no longer gets clipped in themes that letter-space their buttons. Madman's Box moves to 1.1.11.

### Dependencies

Next.js 16.4.6, the OpenAI SDK 7.54, TanStack Query 5.107 and the OpenRouter SDK 1.5.73 lead a general update across the root, the packages and the plugins. The OpenRouter plugin, which 4.12.2 moved onto Chat Completions for streaming, now sends every request that way. Old development images are now deleted from Docker Hub properly instead of merely untagged.

## Repairs

Ten GitHub issues were closed in this cycle, **#146** through **#155**. Most are told above: the lost prompts (#146), the startup heal that blanked vaults (#148), the Clear button that didn't (#149), the scenarios that came back from archive (#150), the silent arrival (#151), and the two places where the New Chat dialog and the server disagreed (#152, #154). The rest, and a few that never got a number:

Moving a folder in a filesystem store, but typing its name in a different case (`notes` for `Notes`), renamed the folder on disk but left Quilltap's records at the old path. On the next scan every file in it was treated as new, losing its chunks, embeddings and descriptions (**#147**). Folder paths now match regardless of case, and documents open in Document Mode follow a folder moved from the Scriptorium, as they already did when a character moved it.

The New NPC dialog sent an uploaded avatar to a character called `undefined`. A character with no physical-prompts file could have her physical description overwritten with an empty one. The greeting's private recall marked memories as recently used outside the one place allowed to do so (**#153**), and the Inventory listed the stores it had consulted in whatever order they happened to answer (**#155**).

## Upgrading

**Three migrations run on first boot**, all quick. They add a per-profile setting for how long tool results stay whole (existing profiles keep 3), the portrait history behind each bubble, and a record of whether each chat's opening has run (existing chats count as already opened). The portrait history starts empty, so earlier lines in existing chats show today's portraits.

**Every existing character is now a roleplay character.** Nothing in your characters is changed, but the kind decides how **Agent Mode** behaves. A character you use for research, coding or other work will now get in-character Agent Mode, which writes prose and never offers *submit final response*, until you set her kind to **assistant** in the Facts card on her page. The Brahma Console and Help chat are unaffected.

**MODERN General is retired.** The built-in copy is removed from the sample prompts. Prompts you copied from it into a character are yours and are not touched. For a story character, MODERN Roleplay is the new recommendation; for a working one, MODERN Assistant. Your existing characters keep their prompts. Run the Optimizer on one you would like brought up to the new standard.

**The Brahma Console's default turn budget is now 10** (it was 50), and the setting no longer has an upper limit. If you never changed it, Brahma will now stop sooner on a long investigation; raise it in the Foundry. A value you saved earlier is kept. The Salon's Agent Mode cap now goes up to 50; its default stays 10.

**Creating a chat through the API no longer writes the greeting.** `POST /api/v1/chats` returns as soon as the chat exists. Opening it in the Salon runs the opening. A script that creates chats and expects a greeting should call `POST /api/v1/chats/<id>?action=open`. The per-seat `imageProfileId` is no longer accepted; the participant object, like the character endpoints, now refuses unknown fields with a 400. The whole-character PUT refuses `systemPrompts` and `scenarios`; use their own endpoints.

**Settings and character links have moved, and the old ones still work.** Settings links now look like `/settings?section=…`, and a character's page is `/aurora/<id>`. Every old `?tab=` link, and `/aurora/<id>/view` and `/edit`, redirect for good. Saved workspace layouts are upgraded when you open them.

**Paused is now Manual.** The turn control's old *Paused* setting is the same thing under a new name.

**A character who chooses her outfit now also chooses what she carries.** If you relied on characters always arriving with their default Inventory, set their outfit mode to something other than *let them choose*.

**Characters without system transparency can now write in their own vaults**, outside the core folders. If you kept notes in such a character's vault that she was not meant to read, move them to a project or group store.

**Some prompt caches rebuild once.** A character's appearance line changed wording, so a character with a physical description rebuilds her cached prompt prefix on her next turn. The group-scene rules changed too, so every multi-character chat's next turn does the same. After that, caching is better than before.

**What backups carry.** Atlas entries and their pictures, a character's kind and birthday, the public flag on progressions, the portrait history and the tool-result setting travel in backups and `.qtap` exports. Older archives restore with the defaults described above. Whether a chat's opening has run is deliberately not backed up: every restored chat counts as already opened.

Node.js 24+ is still required, unchanged from 4.12.

## Installation

### Electron Desktop App

Download the latest `.dmg` (macOS), `.exe` (Windows), or `.AppImage` (Linux) from the [quilltap-shell releases page](https://github.com/foundry-9/quilltap-shell/releases/latest). The window is only ever a front end, and it offers three back ends: **Direct** (the Node server inside Electron, no isolation), **Docker** (the container image behind a native window, which is the recommended setup and the strongest boundary we ship), and **Remote** (any Quilltap URL, including a dev server, an `npx quilltap` instance, or a machine on your network). Requires shell 4.2.0 or later.

### npm (Node 24 required)

```bash
npm install -g quilltap
quilltap
```

Open `http://localhost:3000` in your browser. Requires Node.js 24+, and on Linux glibc 2.35+. First run downloads and caches locally.

### Docker

```bash
docker pull foundry9/quilltap:4.13.0
```

Or use the startup scripts:

```bash
# Linux / macOS
curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/foundry-9/quilltap-server/refs/heads/main/scripts/start-quilltap.sh | bash

# Windows (PowerShell)
irm https://raw.githubusercontent.com/foundry-9/quilltap-server/refs/heads/main/scripts/start-quilltap.ps1 | iex
```

### Standalone Tarball

Available for environments where npm global installs and Docker are both impractical. See the GitHub releases page for download links.
