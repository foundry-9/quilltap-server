*4.13 taught the house what things were. It learned the names of ships and swords, knew a butler from a research assistant, filed its settings by what they did, and raised the curtain on a new chat at once.*

*What 4.13 could not do was let you change how any of it looked. A theme was a zip archive written by hand in a text editor, checked by nothing until it was installed, and tested by reloading the page. The format had grown two ways of saying everything, and the six bundled themes were full of rules aimed at classes that no longer existed. Meanwhile the left rail had grown to nineteen unlabelled icons. And in the stories, characters still declined to know anything they had not been told, and could not remember what they had made up.*

*4.14 is the release in which the house learns to dress itself, and its people learn to tell a story.*

---

Where 4.13 let the house know what it was talking about, 4.14 lets you decide how it looks.

**Calliope's Atelier** is the headline. It is a theme editor inside Quilltap. A theme starts as a draft: blank, or a copy of any theme you have. Every token it can set has a control made for its type, and the whole app wears the draft while you work on it, without touching the theme you are actually wearing. You can add fonts, pictures and icons, write your own CSS in a proper code editor, browse a gallery of every part a theme can style, point at anything on screen to learn what styles it, and ask **Calliope herself** for help. She reads the draft and proposes changes; you try them on, keep them or throw them away. When the draft is right, install it, or export it as a file to share.

Four further bodies of work define the cycle.

**Themes are rebuilt underneath.** Version 2 is now the only theme format. Every token is listed in one registry, every part of the app a theme may style is in one catalogue, and every theme is checked for contrast. The six bundled themes are rebuilt on it, and the two theme packages are retired in favour of the Atelier and `quilltap themes create`.

**The rail is regrouped.** The left rail goes from nineteen icon-only buttons to ten, with four menus (**Conversations**, **Objects**, **Library** and **Studio**) that list their contents by name. The desktop app, from quilltap-shell 4.2.7, offers the same menus in its menu bar.

**Story characters may tell a story.** A roleplay character is now told she may supply plausible detail where nothing contradicts it, that she is played as written, flaws and all, and that her own troubles can outweigh the conversation. What she tells others as fact is remembered as the telling, so the lighthouse keeps the same year from one chat to the next.

**Research takes a handful of steps.** Brahma, the Scenario Builder and two new wizards share a research kit that finds documents in one call and knows when to stop. The Atlas can draft an entry from your stores, and the Wardrobe can design an outfit from a written brief.

A good many more earned their places. **The home page** is rebuilt: a resume card for your latest chat, full chat titles, a wall of portraits and projects as tiles. Roleplay text gains **an Emphasis style** of its own, so Novel narration stands upright and a stressed word is simply italic. Home, project and character pages can wear **a rotating collage** of your own pictures. Pictures drawn by an uncensored profile now **wear a badge**. Automatic **avatar refreshes wait** for the next turn, so a string of changes costs one portrait. **Auto-Configure** reads the providers' own model catalogues before it asks a model to guess. A character you allow it may **read and edit her own memories**. Summon From Lore can **export** what it makes, and the sample characters **Lorian and Riya** are rewritten. And **Claude through OpenRouter**, NanoGPT or any other gateway works in a multi-character chat again.

A few things change in ways worth reading before you upgrade, all under **Upgrading**: a **version 1 theme** will no longer load, and the app will wear the default until you replace it; **`@quilltap/theme-storybook`** and **`create-quilltap-theme`** are retired; a theme that styled a **rail button** by its `data-nav` value will find that item in a menu instead; and the API paths **deprecated in 4.13** are gone.

The thesis is simple: *4.13 taught the house what things are. 4.14 lets you decide how it looks.*

## Calliope's Atelier

### A Workroom for Themes

Open **Studio** and choose **The Atelier**, or press **Design a theme** on the Theme card in **Settings → Appearance**. It opens as a workspace tab on the list of your drafts.

A **draft** is a theme in progress. Press **New theme** and begin from a **blank canvas** (every token at Quilltap's own setting) or a **copy** of any theme you have, bundled ones included. The copy is yours outright and keeps no tie to the original. **Open a theme file** makes a new draft from a `.qtap-theme` bundle, after every check an install would make.

Each draft is an ordinary folder in Quilltap General, under `Themes/<id>/`, holding exactly the files a bundle would. You can open them in the Scriptorium or Document Mode, and a character with document tools can read them. Drafts are kept in backups but left out of `.qtap` exports; to share one, export it from the Atelier.

**Install** packs the draft and installs it like any other bundle, without a restart. Pictures are converted to WebP and TrueType and OpenType fonts to WOFF2 in the installed copy, and files nothing uses are left out and listed. The draft keeps its originals, and its version goes up by one patch so the next install is distinguishable from the last.

Beside Install are two ways to take the draft away as a file, neither of which installs anything or changes its version:

- **Export theme** packs exactly what an install would ship (WebP pictures, WOFF2 fonts, nothing unused) as `<id>-<version>.qtap-theme`, ready to hand to someone else.
- **Export draft** packs every file as you left it, originals included, as `<id>-<version>-draft.qtap-theme`. Opening it with **Open a theme file** gives back the same draft, so it is the way to move a work in progress to another instance.

Both wait until the draft has no errors.

### The House Wears It While You Work

Every change you make is compiled in the browser and shown at once across the whole app: the Salon, the rail, the dialogs, everything. This is a **preview**: the theme you have chosen to wear is not changed, and nothing about the preview is stored. Pause it, and the house goes back to your own theme. A draft with an error keeps showing the last version that compiled.

### Every Token, Its Own Control

The editor lays out all six hundred and sixty settable tokens by level (**Essentials**, **Components**, **Advanced**) and by group, with a section list, a search across every level, and a **Changed only** filter.

- **Typed controls.** A colour gets a colour picker with its contrast shown; a radius can be one value or each corner; a shadow is a list of layers with offsets, blur, spread, colour and inset; a font family is an ordered stack offering the draft's own fonts and the system stacks, with a sample line; a font weight marks the weights the font actually has. Lengths, durations, transitions, opacities, blurs, images, z-indexes and keywords each have theirs.
- **Edit as text.** Every control has a text form. A value the editor cannot read (a gradient, a `calc()`, a data URI) opens in the text form and is left exactly as written until you change it.
- **Light and dark.** Any token can take a separate dark value. A switch shows whichever mode you are working on.
- **Primitives.** A theme can name its own colours and sizes (`brass`, `walnut`, `text-muted`) and have tokens follow them. Add, rename (every reference is rewritten), retype and remove them; removing one shows what will take its value first.
- **Density.** *Compact* or *Comfortable*, for twenty-five sizing tokens at once.
- **Undo and redo** for the whole session, with buttons or Ctrl/Cmd+Z.

On a narrow pane, the section list becomes a menu above the editor, and the field grids rearrange to the width of the pane rather than the window.

### Fonts, Pictures, Icons and the Rest

Below the tokens are the sections for everything else a theme carries:

- **Fonts**, added in batches. The family, weight, style, variable axes and embedding permission are read from the file itself, with a licence (and optional licence file) for the batch.
- **Images**, for page backgrounds and the theme's preview picture. One that is still in use cannot be removed until nothing uses it.
- **Icons.** Any of the app's icons can be overridden with an SVG (tinted by the theme) or a WebP (drawn as is), or reset.
- **Subsystems**, each renamed, described and given its own thumbnail and page picture.
- **Details**: author, licence, tags and whether the theme is dark.
- **Files**: every file in the draft, its size, what uses it, and the bundle's totals against its limits.

### Custom CSS, in a Real Editor

The **Custom CSS** section starts a stylesheet for the draft and edits it in a new code editor with highlighting, line numbers, indenting with Tab and Shift+Tab, and sensible braces: Enter after `{` indents the next line, and Enter between a pair puts the closing brace on its own line. It hands back exactly the bytes you gave it, tabs, line endings and all.

Problems are marked on their lines and listed with a button that jumps to each. Beside the editor is a **reference** of everything a stylesheet may aim at (each catalogued part of the app with its variants and states, every token with its value in force, and the draft's images), and any entry inserts itself at the caret. Rules are written without the theme's id; Quilltap scopes them so they can never reach into a part of the page wearing another theme.

The same editor now opens `.json` and `.css` files in **Document Mode**.

### The Specimen Gallery and the Inspector

**Open the gallery** shows *Calliope's Specimens*: every one of the 232 parts of the app a theme may style, drawn in every variant and state, wearing the draft. Show it as the window does, in light, in dark, or both side by side, and search or jump by surface.

**Inspect** turns your pointer into a magnifying glass. Point at anything in the app and the nearest stylable part is outlined; click, and a panel names it and lists the tokens that style it, grouped by property, with each one's value, where that value came from and which custom rules match. From there you can jump to the token's row, start a CSS rule for the element, or find it in the gallery. Escape puts the glass away.

### Calliope Herself

The Calliope button opens a conversation with **Calliope**, the Atelier's muse. Each draft has its own conversations with her.

She reads the draft, looks up tokens and parts of the app, and checks contrast, and then she **proposes**. A proposal is a card listing the changes: tokens set or reset, primitives added or renamed, CSS replaced. Before you ever see it, Quilltap applies it to a copy of the draft, compiles it as an install would, and compares the result with the draft as it is: new errors and warnings, contrast broken or mended, problems cleared. A proposal that would break the theme goes back to her and is never shown.

- **Try** dresses the preview in the proposal without changing the draft.
- **Keep** applies it through the editor's own editing, as one step you can undo.
- **Discard** sets it aside.

If you have edited the draft since she proposed something, the card says it is stale and offers to ask her again. **Calliope never writes the draft herself.**

You can hand her a question from anywhere in the editor: the inspector, any validation error or warning, any failing contrast line. Each starts a message in her composer for you to finish; nothing is sent until you send it. She cannot see the app, only the draft, so when the draft does not show what you mean she asks for a screenshot, and her composer takes attachments. Her engine and turn budget (25 by default) are on a new **Calliope** card in **Settings → Appearance**.

### Making a Theme

A new help page, **Making a Theme**, walks you through a theme from start to finish: palette, colours, contrast, fonts, images, the gallery and inspector, custom CSS, Calliope, install and export, and the command line for those who prefer it.

## Themes, Rebuilt Underneath

### One Format

A theme used to be able to say the same thing two ways, and the app read whichever it found. **Version 2 is now the only format.** A bundle is a `theme.json` manifest and a `tokens.json` of named primitives and token overrides, plus an optional stylesheet, fonts and images. Quilltap validates and compiles it on the server, checking every token name (with suggestions for near misses), every reference, every type, every file, and the contrast of every pair that matters. Both documents are published as JSON Schemas, and every token is documented in a generated reference.

A **version 1** bundle, or a theme supplied by a plugin, is **refused**: listed on the Appearance card under *Garments Left at the Door* with the reason, a link to help and (for bundles) an Uninstall button. Nothing is deleted. See **Upgrading**.

### One Registry, One Catalogue

Behind the editor are two lists. The **token registry** holds every token a theme may set, with its type, group, default and dark default; the app's own default stylesheets are generated from it. The **hook catalogue** holds every part of the app a theme may aim its CSS at, now 232 of them, from Salon messages and the six roleplay spans to tooltips, empty states and progress bars. Every hook has a specimen in the gallery, and `npm run lint` fails if one goes missing from the app.

### The Bundled Themes

Art Deco, Earl Grey, Great Estate, Madman's Box, Old School and Rains are rebuilt as version 2 bundles. Their colours are now named primitives, and the hand-written rules they carried are replaced by tokens wherever the app already had one: scrollbars, focus rings, text selection, buttons, badges, alerts, heading fonts. Their custom CSS shrinks by between a half and nearly all (Old School no longer needs any). Each now carries the licence for its fonts, read from the font files themselves.

You may notice a few differences, all of them corrections:

- **Earl Grey** now renders in Rethink Sans and **Rains** sets its code in Fira Code, the fonts they always named but never actually applied.
- Scrollbars in Art Deco, Great Estate and Madman's Box take their theme's colours in Chromium too.
- **Old School** now uses the app's keyboard focus ring instead of the browser's.
- The theme preview dialog renders from the compiled theme, so it matches the live app.

Fifty-four contrast failures across the default and bundled themes were fixed by adjusting lightness alone, and later in the cycle form-field edges were raised to 3:1 in every theme and mode. `npm run lint` now holds every bundled theme to every contrast pair, with no exceptions. The bundled themes move to 2.0.1, Madman's Box to 2.0.2.

A theme's custom CSS and icon overrides used to reach into nested elements wearing a different theme, such as the preview cards on the Appearance card. Every such rule is now scoped so it stops at the border.

### From the Command Line

`quilltap themes create <name>` writes a blank version 2 bundle, the same one the Atelier's *New theme* starts from, and no longer needs npm. `quilltap themes validate`, `install` and `export` use the app's own engine, so the command line and the app can never disagree, and the shell now completes bundle paths after `install` and `validate`.

## The Rail, Regrouped

The left rail had grown to nineteen buttons, each a bare icon. It now has ten: **Home**, **Characters**, four menus, then **Help**, **Quick-hide**, **Settings** and your profile.

- **Conversations**: Chats, New Chat…, Scenarios, Brahma Console.
- **Objects**: Wardrobe, Inventory, The Atlas, Pascal's Workbench.
- **Library**: Projects, Files, The Scriptorium, My Photos, Open Document….
- **Studio**: Generate Image, The Atelier, Theme Gallery, Theme….

Each menu opens on a click, lists its items with an icon and a name, and closes on Escape, a click elsewhere or a choice. It works from the keyboard throughout, and on a phone it rises as a sheet from the bottom. An item you cannot use yet (Brahma with no connection profile, say) stays in the list with the reason beside it. Generate Image, the Atelier and the Theme Gallery had no place on the rail before.

The theme picker always lives in **Studio → Theme…** now, so the Appearance toggle that hid it is gone. The help pages give directions the same way ("open **Library** and choose **The Scriptorium**"), and a help-enabled character is asked to do the same.

The rail is built from one list, which Quilltap also serves to the desktop app. **quilltap-shell 4.2.7** builds its menu bar from it, so the desktop app's menus match the rail, and choosing an item there does exactly what clicking it on the rail would. An older shell, or a shell pointed at an older server, keeps its familiar **Navigate** menu.

## Home and the Page

### Home, in Bands

The home page used to be three equal columns clipped to the window: Projects sat half empty, chat titles were cut at about twenty characters, and only eight characters fit. It is now a header line and three bands, each as tall as what it holds, and the page scrolls.

- **The header** puts the greeting and the quick actions on one line. *Continue Last* gives way to the band below.
- **Recent conversations** opens with a **resume card** for your newest chat (its story background, full title, cast, project, a line from its summary and one **Resume** button) beside the six chats before it, each with room for a two-line title and the name of its project. The resume card honours quick-hide, which the old button did not.
- **The Company** is a wall of portraits two rows deep, favourites starred. Click one to start a chat; hover, or reach it from the keyboard, and a card shows the character's title, model, latest chat and a link to her page.
- **Projects** appear as tiles, and the band is left out when you have none.

The bands follow the width of their pane rather than the window, so a split workspace stacks them properly.

### Emphasis Is Its Own Style

Roleplay text had no italics that meant only emphasis. Novel set its narration in italics, so a stressed word had to be bold and italic; Standard told models to use bold for stress. There is now an **Emphasis** style, italic with no colour of its own, which turns upright inside a voice that is already italic, and a **Quiet** style for narration in a softer ink.

- **Novel**: narration is upright in quiet ink, and `*word*` is plain italic.
- **Standard**: `_word_` is the emphasis mark. Models are asked for underscores for stress and bold for force.
- **Quilltap RP**: `*word*` is emphasis, styled inside brackets and braces too.

Both Standard and Quilltap RP gain an **Emph** toolbar button, and the template editor offers Emphasis and Quiet. An underscore mark now follows Markdown's rule, so `snake_case_name` is left alone.

### Easier to See

Selected text was a faint tint, barely distinguishable from the rest; it is now much stronger in the default and bundled themes. In the composer under Novel, narration and dialogue looked alike in Earl Grey and several light modes, because narration was tinted with a colour equal or close to the text itself; it now takes a distinctly cooler hue. And a selected `((aside))` no longer vanishes in a light theme.

## Characters Who Tell a Story

### Latitude to Invent

4.13 gave every character a kind. 4.14 tells a **roleplay** character what that kind allows. Every turn, she now hears three things:

- She may **fill in the world**. Where nothing established contradicts it, she may supply plausible detail: the name of the innkeeper, the year the lighthouse was built, what is in the cellar. What you narrated, what has been said, her memories and real tool results all outrank an invention, and her own system prompt outranks this.
- She is **played as written**. Fear, distrust and bad motives included. A character written with a flaw keeps it.
- She has **troubles of her own**, and they can outweigh the conversation.

An **assistant** hears none of it, and her prompt is unchanged. The rule about recalling past events now differs by kind as well: an assistant still says she does not remember, while a roleplay character still searches first, reports what she finds as it is, and may then fill a real gap, though never in what you said or did. Something done in the story that no tool performs is narration, not a missed tool call.

The **MODERN Roleplay** sample prompt gains a section on filling in the world and two new failure modes to avoid, *the saint* and *the blank*. The prompt generators write flaws as working parts of a character, and the Character Optimizer now treats a flawed character whose memories show her uniformly agreeable as drift to correct.

**Carina** follows the kind too. A roleplay answerer is told to look first and then answer with particulars, rather than saying she does not know. An assistant answerer is unchanged, and **Brahma never invents**: Brahma's rules now separate a fact, which is never made up, from reasoning about facts, which is allowed.

### Remembering the Telling

An invention is only useful if it stays invented. A roleplay character played by a model now forms a memory of what she told others as fact ("I told Sam the lighthouse was built in 1842"), and every Carina answer is remembered as the question and the answer given. The memory is of the *telling*, not the fact, so she remembers having said it rather than coming to believe it. Telling the same account again does not make a second memory, and a changed account replaces the old one.

In testing, an archivist asked about a lighthouse in four separate chats gave four different years. With this change she gave the same one every time.

### Mending Their Own Memories

A character can now be trusted with her own Commonplace Book. Turn on **Edits their own memories** (Aurora, **Abilities → Tools & talents**; off by default) and she is offered two tools on Salon turns. **`read_memory`** takes a memory's id and returns it, along with facts she may read but not change: its kind, its source, its dates, how often it has been reinforced. **`edit_memory`** takes the memory back with her changes. She may revise the text or the summary, change the keywords, raise or lower its importance, say whom it is about, and link it to her other memories. Only the fields she changed are written, and a revised text is re-indexed at once, so the next search finds it as it now reads.

She may touch only her own memories, and never a keystone; those remain yours. The setting is separate from **System transparency**. It needs a connection profile that allows tool calls, and it works in autonomous rooms too.

## Research in a Handful of Steps

### The Research Kit

A review of Brahma Console runs found judgment questions taking up to forty-eight model calls and nearly six minutes, two document tools that failed on every call, and runs that kept searching after saying they had enough.

Brahma, `@Brahma`, the Scenario Builder and the two new wizards below now share a **research kit**:

- **`find_documents`** searches content and names across every store in reach in one call, with one hit per file, a few snippets, its address, and the nearest names when nothing matches.
- **`list_documents`** lists the stores in reach, or outlines one store or folder.
- In the Console, **`lookup_conversations`**, **`lookup_group`**, **`lookup_project`**, **`lookup_wardrobe`** and **`lookup_atlas`** answer the usual questions without SQL, and `lookup_character`, `lookup_memories` and `lookup_messages` return tidier, shorter results. **`lookup_chat_state`** reads a conversation's live state from its records (who is seated, what each carries and wears, the scene and the Concierge posture), and an `@Brahma` question asked in a chat reads that chat by default. Before, asked what a character had equipped right now, Brahma read the object's arrival default and said nothing.
- Shared instructions tell every surface how to classify a request, how many steps it deserves, which tool to reach for first, and when to stop. Gentle reminders arrive if a run checks in too late, says twice that it has enough, or makes the same failing call twice. None ends the run.
- `run_sql` errors now list the columns or tables that do exist.

In testing, Console answers took four to seven model calls and six to twenty-nine seconds. The `doc_grep` and `doc_list_files` tools that always failed in the Console now work, though the kit replaces them where it is offered.

### New Entry from Lore

The Atlas browser has a **New Entry from Lore…** button. Give it a subject and any details, choose whether it may search your stores and records, and Brahma drafts the entry: name, aliases, graphic prompt and knowledge. Revise it with further instructions, then **Open in the editor**. Nothing is saved until you save it there.

### The Atlas as a Lorebook

Atlas knowledge now arrives as **lore** in the Commonplace Book's whisper, ahead of Knowledge, rather than in a separate section at the end of the turn, so you can see it in the whisper too. An entry still rides along when it is **named**, and now also when it is **recalled**: the turn's search finds Atlas entries that match what is being discussed even when nobody names them. Both share Knowledge's budget, which named entries did not count against before. Entries written through the Atlas are now indexed for search at once, and listings sort the way scenario lists do, ignoring a leading "The".

### Import from Instructions

The Wardrobe dialog has a second wizard beside **Import from image**: **Import from instructions**. Write what you want ("a winter outfit for the coaching inn, in keeping with the rest of her wardrobe") and Aurora designs the pieces and an outfit for you to review. She sees the wearer's looks and existing wardrobe and may reuse a piece already there instead of designing a duplicate. Optionally she may search your stores and records, and consult the web. She can read only; she cannot change anything. **Revise** runs again from the design as you have edited it, and nothing is created until you press **Import**. It works in all four tiers.

## Pictures

### Dynamic Backgrounds

A new **Dynamic backgrounds** card under **Settings → Appearance** hangs a rotating collage of your own pictures behind three kinds of page:

- **Home** uses every story background.
- A **project** uses its own story backgrounds, including those of chats moved into it.
- A **character** uses her avatar rolls.

The collage is redrawn every few minutes (three by default, from one to one hundred and twenty), only on the tab you are looking at, and the next one loads before it replaces the last. A page with fewer than three pictures keeps its usual background. Uncensored pictures are left out unless you allow them. On the home tab and project pages, the collage takes precedence over a theme's page background.

### The Uncensored Badge

Every picture Quilltap generates now records whether it was drawn uncensored, and how: an uncensored profile chosen outright, the Concierge routing the call before it ran, or an understudy drawing what an ordinary profile refused. Pictures drawn uncensored wear an **Uncensored** badge, with the reason in its tooltip, in every gallery: the chat gallery, Aurora's album, avatar rolls, Wardrobe pictures, My Photos and the image gallery. A photo kept from such a picture wears its badge too. Pictures made before this release carry no record and no badge.

### Portraits Wait Their Turn

Automatic avatar refreshes, after a wardrobe or Inventory change or a character's arrival, no longer start at once. They wait for the chat's next turn, and later changes for the same character fold into the waiting one, so changing three garments between turns costs one portrait rather than three. The Tasks queue shows them as *Waiting for the next turn*. **Regenerate Avatar** still starts at once.

### Fewer Wasted Backgrounds

In group chats, two story backgrounds often arrived back to back, the first replaced before anyone saw it. The cause was two summary folds running over the same turns, each retitling the chat. A chat now runs one fold at a time.

The stale-chat tidy-up could delete a character's default avatar if that portrait had first been generated in a chat that later went stale. It now checks every place a picture is kept before removing it. Behind that, one generated picture in seven was recording the fingerprint of bytes that were never stored; a migration corrects them.

## Connections

### Auto-Configure Looks It Up

**Auto-Configure** on a connection profile used to ask a model to estimate the context window and output limit. It now looks the facts up first, strongest source first: the provider's live model catalogue read with your own key, the plugin's own table, then OpenRouter's public catalogue. A model is asked only for what is still unknown, and never overrules a fact. It also reports whether the model takes tools and images and recommends a tool format, and the form shows where each value came from. A web search is now optional and runs only if a limit is still unknown. The OpenRouter, NanoGPT, Google and Ollama plugins supply catalogues.

### Claude Through a Gateway

Anthropic's newer models refuse a reply that has been started for them. Quilltap stopped doing that for the Anthropic provider, but a profile reaching Claude through **OpenRouter**, **NanoGPT** or any OpenAI-compatible proxy still did, so every multi-character turn failed. A chat with a user character counts as multi-character, so in practice the greeting failed silently and so did every reply. The setup wizard's own OpenRouter profile was one of them. The default is now off for any model named Claude, whatever the route, and a migration turns it off on existing profiles.

### Smaller Matters

- **The Courier** gets its own card under **Settings → Connections → By provider**, instead of appearing under whichever provider an older Courier profile named.
- **Web search** now counts as configured only when its provider has its API key. Before, the Scenario Builder and other surfaces offered `search_web` and every call failed.
- **The curl plugin** can now be used on a turn limited to reading: GET, HEAD and OPTIONS are allowed, and anything else is refused before it runs. Before, curl was removed from such turns entirely. The Writing Assistant can now fetch pages.
- **`search` sent every result twice.** The model was handed the rendered results and the raw result list beside them, so each memory's text, summary and dates went out twice. It now gets the rendered text alone, and each memory found there shows its **Memory ID**, which `read_memory` takes.
- **OpenAI and Grok** reported a reply cut off at its output limit in a way Quilltap did not recognise, so a tool call cut off halfway could be run with half its arguments. It is now caught and the model asked again (**#228**).

## Smaller Courtesies

- **The formatting toolbar stays in view.** In a long Markdown field, the toolbar now pins to the top of the scrolling area instead of scrolling away.
- **Summon From Lore can export.** The review step offers **Export as .qtap** beside **Import Character**, and again once the import is done, so a summoned character can be kept as a file or taken to another instance. This works in Aurora and from the Salon's Add Character alike.
- **Summon From Lore finishes what it starts.** On models that think before they answer, steps were cut off at their limits and patched over: a system prompt ending mid-sentence, a missing full description, thirteen memories replaced by three in a "repair". Every step now has room to think, a cut-off step fails rather than being patched, and a repair may no longer replace a list with a shorter one. An overlong physical prompt is trimmed at a sentence or word, not mid-word.
- **The scene state no longer takes a character at her word.** The scene tracker reads only prose, so when a character narrated putting something away without actually doing it, her own *Current State* told her she had, beside a record saying she still held it. Actions are now reported as told, attributed to whoever told them, and carried and worn objects are named as the records.
- **Lorian and Riya are rewritten.** The two sample characters keep their names, titles and ids but are now ordinary people in no particular setting: Lorian (Lounès Haddad), a teacher of literature and Latin from Béjaïa, and Riya (Riya Selvam), a mechanical engineer from Chennai and Delhi. Each now has a manifesto, an identity, a full physical description (a new instance's pair had none, because the old seed was in a shape the importer no longer read), a scenario, eight wardrobe pieces and twelve memories, and is told to consult the help tools rather than guess about Quilltap.
- **Reset Built-in Characters keeps their ids.** It used to bring Lorian and Riya back under new ids, leaving the chats that named the old ones pointing at nobody. They now return under the same ids, and a reset that fails says so instead of reporting success.
- **A new thinking icon**: a feathered quill. Themes that override it are unaffected.
- **Brahma counts cold memories correctly.** Its notes on the database said every cold memory had been folded into a digest. Memories demoted past the cap are cold too, and Brahma now tells the two apart.
- **Brahma's activity lines** keep their quotation marks balanced when a search contains a quoted phrase.

## Under the Floorboards

### A Fresh Instance's Second Boot

On a brand-new database, Quilltap's migrations run before the app creates its tables, so every historical data migration found nothing to do and, recording nothing, stayed armed. On the **second** boot, up to eighteen of them ran against data written by the current code. One cleared the sample characters' avatars and removed their gallery rows; others re-attributed memories so that a character's memories about someone else became about herself.

A migration that finds nothing to do is now settled for good when the database was created that boot, or when the migration is older than the release that created the database. Existing instances settle their dormant ones on their next boot. Data already lost this way is not restored.

### Accessibility

Before release the whole app was walked again for keyboard and screen-reader use. Focus rings came back on buttons that also carried a shadow, show under the operating system's high-contrast mode, and no longer draw a white band in dark themes. Six file drop zones that answered only to a mouse now answer to the keyboard. Connection-test results and import errors are announced. Idle queue badges no longer fade their text, and the photo behind the workspace is a little dimmer in dark mode so the text on it holds its contrast.

### Sturdier Underneath

Every `DELETE` route now refuses an `?action=` it does not know with a 400, rather than going ahead and deleting; forty-three of them used to. The API paths deprecated in 4.13 are removed (see **Upgrading**). Thirteen `qt-*` classes that styled nothing are fixed, and lint now fails on any class that resolves to nothing. A failed theme-draft copy no longer leaves a half-made folder holding its name (**#226**). `npm audit` reports no vulnerabilities.

### For Plugin and Theme Authors

`@quilltap/plugin-types` moves to 2.13.0 and `@quilltap/plugin-utils` to 2.7.8. A provider plugin can offer `getModelCatalog(apiKey, baseUrl)`, which Auto-Configure reads. A tool plugin's `getToolEffects` may return an effect that depends on the call (`{ max, of(input), notes? }`), so a tool that is sometimes a read and sometimes a write can stay available under a read-only limit.

**`@quilltap/theme-storybook` and `create-quilltap-theme` are retired.** The specimen gallery and the Atelier replace the first, `quilltap themes create` the second. The authoring guide is `THEME_DEVELOPMENT.md`, the token reference `THEME_TOKENS.md`, and the hook reference `THEME_HOOKS.md`. The hook catalogue is at version 2: rail items that moved into menus are `qt-left-sidebar-menu-item` rows with the same `data-nav` values, and there are new hooks for the menus themselves. New utilities include `qt-inline-link`, `qt-icon-inline-note`, `qt-input-sm` and `qt-uncensored-badge`. New hooks cover the roleplay Emphasis and Quiet styles (`qt-roleplay-emphasis`, `qt-roleplay-quiet`, with three new tokens) and the rebuilt home page (`qt-homepage-header`, `qt-homepage-resume-picture`, `qt-homepage-resume-title`, `qt-character-card-popover`); `qt-character-card` is now an unbordered portrait tile. A theme that is behind the catalogue is warned at load if its CSS still names a hook that has gone.

### Dependencies

`better-sqlite3-multiple-ciphers` 13.2.0, the OpenAI SDK 7.62, the Anthropic SDK 1.12, the OpenRouter SDK 1.5.128 and TanStack Query 5.108 lead a general update across the root, the packages and every plugin. New editor pieces are `@lexical/code-prism` for highlighting, `fontkit` for reading fonts and `wawoff2` for converting them.

## Upgrading

**Version 1 themes no longer load.** A version 1 bundle you installed, or a theme supplied by a plugin, is listed on the Appearance card under *Garments Left at the Door* and is not applied. If it was the theme you wore, Quilltap wears the default and tells you once. Nothing is deleted; uninstall it from the same card when you are ready. A version 1 bundle cannot be opened in the Atelier either. To recreate one, start a new draft (perhaps as a copy of the bundled theme it most resembles) and rebuild it there, or ask its author for a version 2 bundle. The six bundled themes are already version 2.

**`@quilltap/theme-storybook` and `create-quilltap-theme` are retired** and will get no further releases. Use the Atelier, or `quilltap themes create <name>`.

**The rail has changed.** Most destinations are now inside the four menus. An installed theme that styled a single rail button by its `data-nav` value will now find that item as a row in a menu. The *show the theme selector on the rail* preference is gone: **Theme…** is always in the **Studio** menu.

**Six migrations run on first boot**, all quick. They add a column naming the theme draft each Calliope conversation belongs to, a column for each character's **Edits their own memories** permission (off), a column recording whether each generated picture was drawn uncensored (existing pictures are left blank), and the dynamic-backgrounds setting (off). One corrects the fingerprints of generated pictures that recorded the wrong one. The last turns off the `[Name]` prefill on profiles that reach Claude through OpenRouter, NanoGPT or another gateway; on profiles that had it on, multi-character chats start working again. On the same boot, older migrations that a database never needed are settled for good.

**Some prompt caches rebuild once.** Every roleplay character now gets a new section in her prompt, so each one's cached prefix rebuilds on her next turn. After that, caching is as good as before. Assistant characters are unaffected.

**Removed API paths.** The paths that answered for one release after 4.13 moved their actions to `?action=` are gone: `wardrobe/analyze-image`, `wardrobe/preview-avatar`, the `wardrobe`, `atlas` and `inventory` `/transfers` paths, `groups/scenarios`, `system/browse-directory`, `system/plugins/initialize` and `system/plugins/upgrades`. So are the aliases `?action=turn` on a chat and `?action=tasks-queue` on the tools route; use `turn-nudge`, `turn-queue`, `turn-dequeue`, `turn-query` or `turn-skip-user`, and `tasks-queue-start` or `tasks-queue-stop`. An unknown `?action=` on any `DELETE` is now a 400. The replacements are in `docs/developer/API.md`.

**Novel reads differently.** Narration under the built-in Novel template is now upright in a softer ink rather than italic, and emphasis is plain italic rather than bold italic. The built-in templates update on the next start; a copy of Novel you made earlier keeps the old styling until you change it.

**The home page's data has changed shape.** `GET /api/v1/system/home` drops `lastChatId`, adds `project` and `summaryExcerpt` to each chat, `latestChat` to each character and a `characterTotal`, and returns up to 48 characters instead of 24.

**Web search must have its key.** If you have a search plugin installed but never gave it an API key, `search_web` is no longer offered anywhere. Before, it was offered and failed on every call.

**Avatar refreshes now wait for the next turn.** If you change a character's clothes and wait for her new portrait without sending anything, it will not come until the next turn. **Regenerate Avatar** still runs at once.

**A fresh instance from 4.13.** If you set up a new instance on 4.13 and it has since restarted, the sample characters may have lost their avatars, and some seeded memories may be about the wrong character. 4.14 stops this from happening again but does not undo it. **Reset Built-in Characters**, on the toolbar of Aurora's Characters page, puts the sample characters back.

**Lorian and Riya are not changed for you.** An existing instance keeps the sample characters it has; only a new instance seeds the rewritten pair. **Reset Built-in Characters** brings in the new versions under the same ids. Characters reset before 4.14 kept the new ids they were given then; the old ones are not recovered.

**What backups carry.** Theme drafts, a Calliope conversation's link to its draft, each character's memory-editing permission, each generated picture's uncensored record and the dynamic-backgrounds setting travel in backups. Theme drafts are deliberately left out of `.qtap` exports; export a draft from the Atelier as a `.qtap-theme` instead. Older archives restore with the defaults above.

**Desktop app: update to quilltap-shell 4.2.7.** It builds its menu bar from the rail's menus. Older shells keep their own **Navigate** menu and work as before, and 4.2.7 falls back to that menu when it talks to a server older than 4.14.

Node.js 24+ is still required, unchanged from 4.13.

## Installation

### Electron Desktop App

Download the latest `.dmg` (macOS), `.exe` (Windows), or `.AppImage` (Linux) from the [quilltap-shell releases page](https://github.com/foundry-9/quilltap-shell/releases/latest). The window is only ever a front end, and it offers three back ends: **Direct** (the Node server inside Electron, no isolation), **Docker** (the container image behind a native window, which is the recommended setup and the strongest boundary we ship), and **Remote** (any Quilltap URL, including a dev server, an `npx quilltap` instance, or a machine on your network). Requires shell 4.2.0 or later; 4.2.7 or later is recommended, since it builds its menu bar from the rail's menus and keeps the server on localhost unless you tick **Open to the network** on the splash screen.

### npm (Node 24 required)

```bash
npm install -g quilltap
quilltap
```

Open `http://localhost:3000` in your browser. Requires Node.js 24+, and on Linux glibc 2.35+. First run downloads and caches locally.

### Docker

```bash
docker pull foundry9/quilltap:4.14.0
```

Or use the startup scripts:

```bash
# Linux / macOS
curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/foundry-9/quilltap-server/refs/heads/main/scripts/start-quilltap.sh | bash

# Windows (PowerShell)
irm https://raw.githubusercontent.com/foundry-9/quilltap-server/refs/heads/main/scripts/start-quilltap.ps1 | iex
```

### Standalone Tarball

Available for environments where npm global installs and Docker are both impractical. See the GitHub releases page for download links.
